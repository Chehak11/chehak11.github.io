Lead-screw robotic gripper

A two-finger gripper in which a central lead screw drives mirrored four-bar linkages to close the fingertips. I adapted an existing design and modeled it in Onshape as a Yonder Dynamics design task.

How it works

A motor turns the central lead screw, which drives a carriage up and down. The carriage pushes mirrored four-bar linkages on either side, which swing the two triangular fingertips together or apart.

Why a lead screw

  • Mechanical advantage: a lead screw turns a small motor torque into a large linear force, so a small motor can still grip firmly.
  • Holding without power: a lead screw with a low enough lead angle is self-locking, so the gripper stays closed when the motor is off.
  • Trade-off: it closes slowly compared with a direct servo drive. That's fine for picking up samples, but not for fast grabs.

My role

The concept is an existing design. I adapted it, modeled it in Onshape, and built the motion study. It hasn't been manufactured.
