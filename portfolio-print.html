JELIX: jellyfish-inspired underwater propulsion

A propeller-free propulsion mechanism for small underwater vehicles. A cam–follower drive with spring return snaps four ribs inward to push out a jet of water, copying the pulse of Cotylorhiza tuberculata. I designed the cam in CATIA V5 and simulated it in MSC ADAMS, supervised by Theja K V.

65 mmrib-tip travel X
80 mmrib-tip travel Y
0–40°rib stroke
12 scycle at 5 rpm
Cutaway: motor and gear train drive the cam; the ribs are pivoted followers.

How it works

  • Expand: the cam slowly raises the ribs and compresses the springs.
  • Contract: the cam profile drops, the springs snap the ribs back, and a jet of water pushes the vehicle.

Analysis

  • Cam profile designed at 10° increments in CATIA V5.
  • MSC ADAMS multibody simulation at 5 rpm over 24 s.
  • Free-body model: Fy on each rib produces thrust.

Why a cam

One motor, low voltage, simple to build and maintain. Smart materials need more complex electronics.

Status: simulation only, not yet built. Next: model the springs and the hydrodynamics, then print a one-rib rig and compare it against simulation.

DWG 04 · Adapted design · Onshape · Oct 2026

Lead-screw robotic gripper

A central lead screw drives a carriage that pushes mirrored four-bar linkages, swinging two fingertips together. I adapted an existing design and modeled it with a motion study in Onshape as a Yonder Dynamics design task.

Why a lead screw

  • High force from a small motor.
  • Can be self-locking, so it holds without power.
  • Trade-off: slower to close than a direct servo.
Status: CAD and motion study; not manufactured. Motion video on the portfolio site.
